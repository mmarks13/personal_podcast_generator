## The computer inside the model
A: Something fun before we go. I saw you looking at it.
B: You did. Someone built a working computer inside a language model. Not a tool call. They took a stock Qwen three point five, the four-billion-parameter model, and compiled a thirty-two-bit RISC-V processor directly into its weights. The author calls it Qwenputer, and the LocalLLaMA forum loved it.
A: Compiled. Not trained.
B: No training at all. In the author's words, "a compiler sets every weight directly." Eleven extra layers sit in front of Qwen's thirty-two, and the processor's memory is the model's own internal state. Every instruction produces a few tokens, and those tokens are the processor running. The author says it passes the official RISC-V test suite and ran eighteen million instructions without an error.
A: Does it help the model?
B: On one long-division test set, the author reports ninety-seven and a half percent, against seventy-two and a half for plain Qwen. It's slow. A big calculation takes seconds, not milliseconds. And the checkpoint doubles in size, mostly zeros.
A: [wry] I'll admit to a strange feeling about that one. A whole computer folded into the weights, and the model doesn't have to know it's there.
