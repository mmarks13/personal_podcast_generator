## Reading the architecture off the clock
A: So this is a measurement paper and it is almost entirely a methods story. ## Two point eight trillion parameters off a hard drive
B: Right. colibri. The pitch in the repository description is one line: run frontier mixture-of-experts models on hardware you already own — pure C, zero dependencies, experts streamed from disk.
A: Streamed from disk.
B: Streamed from disk. And the reason that isn't insane is what a mixture-of-experts model actually is. The parameter count is enormous, but for any given token only a small handful of experts fire. So the model is huge and the working set is small.
A: The model is large and mostly asleep.
B: Mostly asleep. So colibri treats storage, system memory and video memory as one memory hierarchy instead of three places a model either fits or doesn't. The dense parts — attention, shared experts — stay resident in RAM. The routed experts live on disk and get pulled in on demand. There's a least-recently-used cache, there's a learned hot-store that pins the experts your traffic actually keeps hitting, and there's a prefetch thread that predicts what the next layer needs one step ahead.
A: Alan, this is demand paging.
B: [laughs] It is completely demand paging.
A: And that's not a put-down, so let me say exactly what I mean by it. Virtual memory was solved in the sixties. Denning's working-set model is nineteen sixty-eight. And the entire history of that idea is people discovering that their working set was bigger than they thought, and the machine spending all its time fetching instead of computing. That's thrashing, and it's the only failure mode this design has.
B: Right, and the whole bet is that routing gives you a small stable working set. If your traffic keeps hitting the same experts, the hot-store catches it and you're fast. If your routing is diffuse — different experts every token — you're reading from a disk on every layer and you will feel it.
A: So performance depends on the shape of your workload in a way it doesn't for a model that simply fits in memory.
