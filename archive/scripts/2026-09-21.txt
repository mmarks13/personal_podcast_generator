## Half the tokens
B: My paper. N-C-P ArchPreview, out of the Intern team at Shanghai AI Laboratory, twenty-seven authors on it including Dahua Lin and Bowen Zhou, posted to arXiv on the ninth. Three hundred and twenty-six upvotes on Hugging Face, number one paper of its day, and eighteen model variations already citing it — which for a ten-day-old technical report is real adoption, not applause.
A: Start with what it's doing differently, because "beyond next-token prediction" is a sentence I've heard for fifteen years.
B: Right, and most of the time it means "we bolted on a second loss." This is that, but the second loss is a strange one. Alongside ordinary next-token prediction, the model also learns Next Concept Prediction — predicting a discrete concept that spans multiple tokens.
A: Define concept, because that word is doing enormous work.
B: [laughs] It is, and the definition is more modest than the word. They build what they call a product-quantized concept vocabulary out of the model's own hidden states. So: take the hidden representations the model is already producing, chop the vector into chunks, and learn a small codebook for each chunk. Any hidden state becomes a short sequence of codebook indices. That finite set of indices is the concept vocabulary.
A: So a concept is a cluster in its own representation space. Not a thing anyone wrote down.
B: Nobody wrote it down. It's discovered, and the discovery is cheap, which is the trick. Then a Concept Module predicts what the next one of those will be, and — this is the part that makes it more than a regularizer — the predicted future concept is fed back in to guide the token-level generation.
A: So it's forecasting where the sentence is going in its own vocabulary, and then using the forecast.
B: That's it. Write it in the compressed language, then write it in words.
A: And the number?
