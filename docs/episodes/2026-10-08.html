llama.cpp's cache for experts that don't fit

Alan: Last one, and it's a gift. The llama dot c-p-p project merged a pull request yesterday that adds a G-P-U cache for mixture-of-experts models.

Ada: Explain the problem first. Why do experts not fit?

Alan: A mixture-of-experts model is huge on disk but only uses a few experts for each token. On a home machine the experts usually don't fit in the graphics card's memory, so you park most of them in ordinary system memory. Then every token, the experts it needs get computed slowly on the C-P-U, or shipped over to the card.

Ada: And the cache keeps the popular ones on the card.

Alan: Exactly. The pull request says it keeps a least-recently-used cache of experts on the G-P-U, and "only the misses are uploaded." It turns out some experts get picked a lot more than others, so a small cache catches most of the traffic. You turn it on with a flag that sets the cache size, and the authors suggest starting around ten percent of total expert size.

Ada: Numbers?

Alan: Their test: Qwen three point eight Flash Next, quantized to about ninety-four gibibytes, sixty-five of which are experts. On an R-T-X forty-ninety, twenty-five tokens per second without the cache. Thirty-nine point four with a six and a half gigabyte cache, a seventy-two percent hit rate. About one point six times faster. On a fifty-ninety they report up to two point two times.

Ada: For a free flag.

Alan: For a free flag. The caveats are real. It only applies to small batches, thirty-two tokens or fewer, so it's a single-user speedup, not a server one. And your hit rate depends on what you're asking. A model that bounces across domains will miss more.

Ada: So it's the oldest trick in the book, applied to experts. Keep what you used recently close at hand.

Alan: It is. And every operating system that ever paged memory would recognize it. What's new is that the experts are skewed enough for it to pay. That's something I'd like to see someone measure across tasks. Which experts are hot when you're coding, versus when you're chatting.
