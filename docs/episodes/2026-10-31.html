How a model gets copied

Ada: Okay. Distillation. The authors are Kyle Miller and Colin Shea-Blymyer, at C-SET, published on the twenty-sixth. And I'll say what they say up front: it's meant to inform legislative proposals and the policy debate, but it doesn't name a bill, and it deliberately doesn't settle the argument. It's a map.

Alan: A map of what, exactly? Because "distillation" means three different things depending on who's yelling.

Ada: That's the first useful thing it does. Distillation, in general, is training a weaker student model on a stronger teacher model's outputs. There are two flavors. White-box means you have the teacher's insides. Its full scores over every possible next word, its activations, its weights. You're learning from the teacher's whole state of mind. C-SET sets that aside.

Alan: And black-box is you only get the answer.

Ada: You only get the answer. The teacher is closed. You can only reach it the way a customer does, through a website or an A-P-I. And C-SET's term for the fight in Washington is adversarial distillation: Chinese labs doing that to American proprietary models, through third parties, while hiding where the traffic comes from.

Alan: Fine. Walk me through how you'd actually do it.

Ada: Five steps. One, decide what skill you want to transfer, and pick your student and teacher. Two, gather seed prompts. Questions that represent the skill. Benchmarks, custom prompts, even your own model's outputs. Three, steer the teacher. Ask it to answer the seeds and show its reasoning, or to spin each seed into many similar ones, or to grade your student's attempts. Four, do that at scale, programmatically. Five, train the student on what you collected. Either straight supervised fine-tuning, just imitate the answers, or something with feedback, a reward model, reinforcement learning, on-policy distillation.

Alan: Here's my first objection. The usual reason to distill is to make something small and cheap. These Chinese models aren't small.

Ada: That's C-SET's point exactly, and I think it's the most clarifying line in the piece. The students here have hundreds of billions to trillions of parameters. DeepSeek's R1 is over six hundred seventy billion. That's not compression. C-SET's read is that these labs can build a capable base model themselves. What they want is the finishing school. Post-training. The reasoning style, the polish.

Alan: Which is why everyone's fighting over the reasoning traces.

Ada: Right. Public reporting, C-SET says, centers on getting the teacher to show its chain of thought.
