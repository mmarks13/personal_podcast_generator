Twenty-one ways to shrink the same model

Ada: Right, so this is the other end of the telescope entirely. One person, one RTX 5080, sixteen gigabytes.

Alan: What's he measuring? Because "which quant is best" usually means somebody ran a multiple-choice benchmark eight times.

Ada: He isn't measuring accuracy. He's measuring KL divergence against the uncompressed model, and that's why I wanted to talk about it.

Alan: Explain it for me the way you'd explain it to a room.

Ada: The full-precision model, at each position in the text, produces a probability distribution over every possible next token. Not a choice — a distribution. Ten percent here, three percent there, a long tail of almost-nothing. Quantize it, and you get a different distribution at that same position. KL divergence is the number that says how far apart those two distributions are, averaged over all the tokens in the test.

Alan: So zero means the compressed model is thinking the same thoughts.

Ada: Zero means it's producing the same distribution. And I like it as a metric precisely because it doesn't let a model hide. A benchmark score can survive real damage — the compressed model gets the same answer through a distribution that's been mangled everywhere it doesn't happen to matter. Divergence sees the mangling.

Alan: Alright, numbers.

Ada: Worst in his table: a two-bit quantization-aware build, mean divergence zero point eight nine, eight point two gigs on disk. Best top-token agreement in that neighborhood is eighty-six percent. Move up the table: an IQ2-S build at zero point five one. Then here's the one. A build called Ridge, three point seven bits per weight, eleven point seven gigabytes — divergence zero point four eight. And an IQ3 build, nine point four gigabytes, two and a third gigs smaller — zero point three eight.

Alan: The smaller file is closer to the original.

Ada: The smaller file is closer to the original. Bits per weight is not fidelity. What matters is where you spend them — which layers keep precision, which tensors get crushed, whether the recipe was calibrated. Two files can advertise the same average and be entirely different objects.

Alan: That's genuinely useful, and it's the thing everybody gets wrong when they pick a download by file size.

Ada: His overall pick is a four-bit IQ4-X-S build from bartowski, with a couple of alternates for people who want a less filtered model.
