Half the tokens

Alan: My paper. N-C-P ArchPreview, out of the Intern team at Shanghai AI Laboratory, twenty-seven authors on it including Dahua Lin and Bowen Zhou, posted to arXiv on the ninth. Ada: Start with what it's doing differently, because "beyond next-token prediction" is a sentence I've heard for fifteen years.

Alan: Right, and most of the time it means "we bolted on a second loss." This is that, but the second loss is a strange one. Alongside ordinary next-token prediction, the model also learns Next Concept Prediction — predicting a discrete concept that spans multiple tokens.

Ada: Define concept, because that word is doing enormous work.

Alan: It is, and the definition is more modest than the word. They build what they call a product-quantized concept vocabulary out of the model's own hidden states. So: take the hidden representations the model is already producing, chop the vector into chunks, and learn a small codebook for each chunk. Any hidden state becomes a short sequence of codebook indices. That finite set of indices is the concept vocabulary.

Ada: So a concept is a cluster in its own representation space. Not a thing anyone wrote down.

Alan: Nobody wrote it down. It's discovered, and the discovery is cheap, which is the trick. Then a Concept Module predicts what the next one of those will be, and — this is the part that makes it more than a regularizer — the predicted future concept is fed back in to guide the token-level generation.

Ada: So it's forecasting where the sentence is going in its own vocabulary, and then using the forecast.
