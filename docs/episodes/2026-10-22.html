Listening to plants

Alan: To finish, plants. Tim De Chant at TechCrunch wrote about a startup called Electric Plant. The idea started when the chief executive, Justin Mast, saw research on a houseplant wired to a motorized stand. When the plant needed light, electrical signals moved through its tissue, a control board picked them up, and the stand rolled the plant toward the light.

Ada: The plant drove itself to the window.

Alan: More or less. His co-founder is Lee von Kraus, a neuroscientist who's worked with DARPA. The hardware is the part I like. A sensor about the size of an iPhone folded lengthwise. Data goes over a low-power wireless network to a base station, the company says a couple of sensors per acre is enough, and Mast says they cost "tens of dollars," where existing tools cost thousands.

Alan: They call it Bombadil. About three years of collecting data, signals from around a hundred twenty-five species, ferns, redwoods, tomatoes, dahlias.
